Party Wall Services

Not sure if your building work falls under the Party Wall Act?

Whether you're extending your home, converting the loft, or working near a shared boundary, you may have a legal duty to notify your neighbours before starting.

We make the process straightforward, handling everything from advice and notices to inspections and final agreements, so you can focus on your project with peace of mind.

Do I Need to Serve a Party Wall Notice?

If you're planning to:

  • Build a front, rear or side extension
  • Convert your loft with steel beams
  • Excavate to lay new foundations for a new structure or basement
  • Alter or remove a chimney breast
  • Build or work on a wall that sits on a boundary line

You may be legally required to serve a Party Wall Notice to your neighbours before work begins. Our team of Chartered Surveyors can advise you on your responsibilities, prepare the necessary notices, and act on your behalf throughout the process, helping to keep your project running smoothly and legally.

What Happens if I've Been Served a Party Wall Notice?

If your neighbour has sent you a Party Wall Notice, don’t worry, this is a normal part of many types of building work.

As an Adjoining Owner, you have the right to:

  • Appoint your own surveyor (usually at your neighbour’s expense)
  • Request a record of your property’s condition before works begin
  • Have any concerns addressed before work starts
  • Be protected from damage during and after the project

We act on behalf of Adjoining Owners to ensure your property is properly assessed, your rights are upheld, and you're not left out of the process.

About the Party Wall etc. Act 1996

The Party Wall etc. Act 1996 is legislation designed to prevent and resolve disputes between neighbouring property owners when certain building works take place near or on shared boundaries.

It applies when a Building Owner intends to carry out:

  • Works directly on or near a boundary line
  • Alterations to a shared (party) wall or structure
  • Excavations near an adjoining building

The Act sets out a clear process for notification, agreement, and dispute resolution, ensuring construction works can proceed while protecting both parties’ interests.
